# CHM 2211: Organic Chemistry II

Virtual labs for a college-level Organic Chemistry II course. Everything runs in a web browser, with nothing to install and no account to create.

**Students:** these labs are linked from the course in Canvas, and that is the best place to open them, because Canvas gives you your own unknown and shows when each one is due.

## Exam 1 Unknown

Each student is given an unknown compound, by a five-character code at the top of the page, and works out its structure from its spectra on a virtual instrument room.

| Part | What the student does |
| --- | --- |
| 1. At the bench | Makes up an NMR sample in CDCl₃, sets its depth in the spinner and lowers it into the magnet; records an IR spectrum on an ATR crystal; makes up a GC vial and injects it by hand into a GC-MS |
| 2. The formula | Works out the degrees of unsaturation from the molecular formula |
| 3. The spectrometer | Locks, shims, records ¹H, ¹³C and DEPT-135 spectra, then processes them: Fourier transform, phase, baseline, reference, peak picking and integration |
| 4. What the spectra show | Reads every ¹H and ¹³C signal into tables, which the page checks |
| 5. The IR spectrum | Chooses the scans and resolution, records a background and the spectrum on a diamond ATR (the crystal must first be wiped of the last user's silicone grease; some students find the IR still testing itself), then reads the important bands |
| 6. The GC-MS | Runs the instrument's software: checks its status, runs the EI Check (some students meet a vented, leaking or wet instrument), sets and saves the method, sets up the run and presses Start on the GC. Then reads the retention time, the molecular ion, the base peak and the main fragments |
| 7. Your structure | Draws the structure, names it, and explains how each spectrum supports it |
| 8. Turn in | Prints the report, or saves it as a PDF, with every spectrum recorded |

The structure itself is not checked by the page: the instructor marks it.

A [guide to running the NMR](https://neogio73.github.io/CHM2211/exam1-unknown/guide/how-to-run-the-nmr.pdf) (PDF), linked from the lab page, takes students through every step from making up the sample to processing the spectra, with pictures of the lab itself. Its spectra come from a practice sample, not from any of the unknowns.

A [prelab review](https://neogio73.github.io/CHM2211/exam1-unknown/prelab/), also linked from the lab page, covers degrees of unsaturation, why each step at the NMR matters, and how to read ¹H, ¹³C with DEPT-135, IR and mass spectra (with an isotope-pattern simulator that shows what +1 and +2 elements do to M⁺, from the natural abundances), with two practice compounds worked from formula to structure and questions to check yourself. Its practice compounds are not among the unknowns.

The spectra are drawn from published data: the NIST Chemistry WebBook, nmrshiftdb2, the Human Metabolome Database, SDBS (National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology, Japan) as held by those two databases, and the chemical literature.

## Grignard Prelab (preview, being built)

A prelab in which students rehearse the microscale Grignard synthesis of triphenylmethanol before doing it at the bench. It is a preview of work in progress and is not yet assigned. What it has so far:

| Stage | What the student does |
| --- | --- |
| Drying the glassware | Looks closely at each piece of glassware, and dries any that still shows water: a rinse of acetone from the wash bottle, or the drying oven at 110 °C, on a bench clock |
| Assembling the apparatus | Builds the handout's apparatus from microscale glassware (a Kimble Microflex kit): the 20-mL round-bottom flask just above the stirring hot plate, a Claisen head clamped at its neck, a septum with the syringe through it on the straight arm, the CaCl₂ drying tube on the side arm, and no condenser. Glass threads are joined by blue couplers, screwed on by the student. Pieces that do not belong are refused, with the reason |

As at the bench, the page does not say whether a choice was right: what the student did is recorded, and its consequences are to show later in the run.

Pointing at anything on the bench shows its name, and a section under the bench pictures and describes each piece of glassware and equipment, without saying where it goes.

The fume hood is the model "Dm3-004-08" by foxart3d, used under CC BY 4.0.
